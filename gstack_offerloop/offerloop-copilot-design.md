# Design: Offerloop Outreach Copilot — From Contact Database to Outreach Operating System

## Problem Statement

The job market is broken for students. Firms receive 12,000+ applicants per role. The only path that reliably works is personal connections + demonstrated genuine interest through cold outreach. Students need to network their way in, but they have no system for doing personalized outreach at scale.

Offerloop exists to solve this, but today it's perceived as a contact database with AI features bolted on. Users pay for email data but could use Rocket Reach or similar. The AI-generated emails require heavy editing. Features (job board, contact search, email drafting, coffee chat prep) are disconnected islands. Users are underwhelmed.

The goal: transform Offerloop from "tool that finds emails" into "system that runs your entire outreach process."

## Demand Evidence

- Paying customers on Pro and Elite tiers
- Users pay for the contact database (finding people's emails) despite underwhelming overall experience
- Market data: 70-80% of jobs fill through networking/referrals, never posted publicly
- Student applications per job up 64% year-over-year (Handshake 2024-2025 data)
- Founder has direct knowledge of the consulting/IB/tech recruiting process and its brokenness

## Status Quo

Students currently cobble together: LinkedIn stalking for contacts, Rocket Reach / similar for emails, ChatGPT for drafting outreach, spreadsheets for tracking who they've contacted, and manual Gmail for sending and follow-ups. This takes 15-20 hours/week and produces generic, obviously-AI-generated messages that get ignored.

Offerloop partially improves this with its contact database and email generation, but users still have to manually connect the dots: find a job → search for contacts → draft an email → send it → track responses. Each step is a separate page with no continuity.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** College students (juniors/seniors) and recent grads breaking into consulting, investment banking, and tech. They know networking matters but don't know how to do it systematically. They're willing to pay $15-35/month for something that actually works.

**Narrowest wedge:** Personalized outreach emails. If the emails are good enough to send without heavy editing, everything else follows. If they're not, nothing else matters.

## Constraints

- Two-person team (Nick + co-founder)
- Co-founder is currently rebuilding email quality (Phase 1 in progress)
- Existing codebase: Flask backend, React frontend, Chrome extension. 32 API blueprints, extensive integrations (PDL, Hunter, OpenAI, Gmail, Stripe, SerpAPI)
- Must not break existing paying users' workflows during transition
- Resume database collection is a strategic priority — onboarding must capture this data
- Deployed on Render, single service

## Premises

1. **Value shifts from data to workflow.** Offerloop is not a contact database. It's a copilot that runs the student's entire outreach process.
2. **Personalization quality is the moat short-term.** Emails must be send-ready, not "here's a draft you need to rewrite." The edit rate is the key metric.
3. **Onboarding is the critical moment.** Resume + interests + LinkedIn = everything downstream. Job matching, contact selection, and email quality all depend on how well you understand the student upfront.
4. **The AI orchestrates, features serve.** Users still see distinct features, but the AI decides what to surface and when. The student shouldn't have to figure out the next step.
5. **Resume database is the long-term moat.** Every onboarded user compounds the proprietary dataset. Volume comes from product quality.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent reviewed this cold, without seeing the conversation. Key findings:

**Steelman:** "Offerloop is building the first outreach operating system purpose-built for students breaking into high-finance and consulting... collapsing what currently takes 20 hours/week into a 10-minute daily review queue."

**Key insight:** The "approve, approve, approve" vision means you're building an agent, not a tool. But an approval button on a mediocre email is worse than no button. "If you ship the pipeline UI before emails are send-ready, you've built a conveyor belt that delivers disappointment faster."

**Challenged premise:** Resume database as moat. Subagent argued student resumes are low-information and the real moat is the behavioral feedback loop (which emails got replies, from which contacts, at which firms). Founder disagreed — resume data has broader market value beyond the current product. Premise kept as-is.

**Prototype suggestion:** "Daily Outreach Queue" — card-stack approval flow. Track edit rate as the key metric. 40%+ unedited sends = business works. Under 15% = go back to prompt engineering.

**Critical metric added by subagent:** Edit rate. Percentage of emails approved without editing. This is the single number that determines whether the copilot vision is viable.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: "Smart Glue" — AI Orchestration Over Existing Features
Keep every feature. Add an AI layer that connects them into a guided flow. Features stay as separate pages, but AI tells you where to go next.

- Effort: M (4-6 weeks)
- Risk: Medium
- Pros: Leverages everything built, fastest path to "feels like a copilot," lower engineering risk
- Cons: Seams show, email quality still bottlenecked, incremental not transformative
- Reuses: Scout, job board, contact search, email generation, all current routes

### Approach B: "The Pipeline" — One Unified Outreach Flow
Single new flow: matched jobs in a feed, each showing contacts + pre-drafted emails inline. One click to review, one click to send. Job board, contact search, and email drafting collapse into one screen.

- Effort: L (6-10 weeks)
- Risk: Medium-High
- Pros: The product as envisioned — jobs to contacts to emails to send to track. Dramatically simpler mental model. Email quality front and center.
- Cons: Significant frontend rebuild, risk of breaking what works, longer to ship
- Reuses: Backend APIs (job board, PDL, email gen, Gmail), onboarding, resume parsing. Frontend mostly new.

### Approach C: "Email-First Rebuild" — Fix the Core, Then Expand
Don't touch the flow. Fix email quality using resume data + job context + contact background to produce genuinely send-ready emails. Then build the connected flow.

- Effort: S-M (2-4 weeks for email quality, then build toward B)
- Risk: Low
- Pros: Addresses the root problem, ships fast, de-risks Approach B
- Cons: Doesn't change UX immediately, users may not notice quality improvement without flow changes
- Reuses: All existing infrastructure, primarily changes email generation prompts and services

## Recommended Approach

**Three phases: Email Quality → Pipeline → Autopilot.**

### Phase 1: Email Quality (Approach C) — In Progress
Co-founder is rebuilding email generation now. The emails must be personalized enough that users approve without editing. This is the foundation everything else depends on.

**Key deliverables:**
- Rebuild email generation prompts to use resume data + job context + contact background
- Three-step generation: pull context → generate → self-critique rewrite
- Eliminate AI-slop signals: no "impressed," "fascinated," "intrigued." No perfect grammar walls of text. Emails should read like a sharp student wrote them, not an AI.
- Target metric: 40%+ of emails approved without editing

**Key files likely touched:**
- `backend/app/services/openai_client.py` — prompt chains
- `backend/app/routes/emails.py` — email generation endpoints
- `backend/app/services/` — any email-related services

### Phase 2: The Pipeline (Approach B) — Next
Once emails are send-ready, build the unified outreach flow.

**Key deliverables:**
- Dashboard with recommended jobs surfaced based on onboarding data (resume + interests + target firms/industries)
- Job cards that show matched contacts inline (alumni prioritized)
- Pre-drafted emails visible per contact, ready to approve/edit/skip
- One-click send (Gmail draft creation only in Phase 2 — direct send deferred to Phase 3 when trust is established)
- Outbox tracking (phased): "sent" and "replied" status in Phase 2 (replied detection via existing Gmail Pub/Sub webhooks). "Opened" tracking (pixel-based, unreliable in Gmail) and "meeting booked" detection deferred to Phase 3.
- Existing features (coffee chat prep, interview prep, resume tools) become accessible from the pipeline but are not the primary flow

**Credit model for pipeline:** The current tier system (300/1500/3000 credits) needs to map to pipeline actions. Auto-matching jobs and surfacing contacts should be free (it's the product working). Credits consumed when: drafting a personalized email (uses AI), sending via Gmail, or doing a new contact lookup. Batch operations consume credits per-item, not per-batch. Tier limits may need restructuring once pipeline usage patterns are clear — track credit burn rate in Phase 2 and adjust before Phase 3.

**Chrome extension in the new world:** The Chrome extension (LinkedIn scraping, job board injection) feeds INTO the pipeline. LinkedIn profile data enriches the student's profile and contact context. Job board scrapers can push discovered jobs into the pipeline queue. The extension shifts from a standalone tool to a data collection layer for the pipeline.

**Route migration plan:**

| Current route | Phase 2 behavior |
|--------------|-----------------|
| `/find` | Becomes the pipeline (primary view) |
| `/job-board` | Embedded in pipeline job feed |
| `/tracker` | Pipeline tracking section replaces this |
| `/contact-directory` | Remains accessible, secondary |
| `/coffee-chat-prep` | Accessible from pipeline job cards |
| `/interview-prep` | Accessible from pipeline job cards |
| `/write/resume`, `/write/cover-letter` | Remain as standalone tools |
| `/application-lab` | Merges into pipeline tracking (overlaps with outbox) |
| `/account-settings`, `/onboarding` | Unchanged |

**Dashboard vision:**
- Student logs in → sees "Your Outreach Queue" with recommended jobs
- Each job card: company, role, 2-3 contacts with emails, draft messages
- Approve / Edit / Skip per email
- Batch send capability
- Pipeline tracking below: where each outreach stands

**Key files likely touched:**
- New page/component for the pipeline flow (frontend)
- `connect-grow-hire/src/` — new pipeline components
- `backend/app/routes/job_board.py` — enhanced job matching
- `backend/app/services/pdl_client.py` — contact lookup per job
- `backend/app/routes/emails.py` — batch email operations
- `backend/app/services/gmail_client.py` — send flow
- `connect-grow-hire/src/contexts/` — pipeline state management

### Phase 3: Full Autopilot — Later
Once the pipeline works and users trust the email quality, add AI orchestration.

**Key deliverables:**
- System proactively finds new job matches daily and pre-populates the queue
- Automated follow-up scheduling (user approves the sequence, system sends on schedule)
- Inbox monitoring: detect replies, surface them, suggest responses
- Full copilot mode: "Approve all" for users who trust the system
- Progressive trust ladder: manual review → batch approve → full autopilot
- AI-suggested outreach timing based on behavioral data

**The Claude Code model:** The student opens Offerloop once a day. The system has already found new jobs, identified contacts, drafted emails. Student reviews, approves, done. 10 minutes. System handles follow-ups, tracks replies, schedules meetings.

## Open Questions

1. **Job board data quality:** Current job board is "extremely weak" per founder. How to source better job data for the recommended jobs feed? Current sources (Fantastic.jobs, Greenhouse, etc.) may not be sufficient for consulting/IB roles specifically.
2. **Gmail send vs. draft:** At what point does the product transition from creating Gmail drafts to actually sending? What's the trust threshold?
3. **Onboarding redesign scope:** Current onboarding captures some data, but the new flow requires richer input (target firms, industries, role types, networking goals). How much friction is acceptable?
4. **Edit rate baseline:** What's the current edit rate on AI-generated emails? Need to measure before Phase 1 improvements to track progress.
5. **Batch sending and deliverability:** Sending 50 personalized emails from a student's Gmail raises deliverability concerns. Rate limiting, domain warmup, and spam prevention need a strategy.
6. **Feedback loop instrumentation:** When do you start tracking which emails get replies? This is the behavioral data that the independent reviewer identified as the strongest potential moat. Earlier is better.
7. **Existing user migration:** How to transition current paying users (who use it as a contact database) to the new pipeline flow without breaking their workflow?

## Success Criteria

- **Phase 1:** Email edit rate improves to 40%+ (emails approved without editing)
- **Phase 2:** Time from "student logs in" to "outreach sent" drops below 10 minutes. User activation improves (users who send 5+ emails in first week).
- **Phase 3:** Daily active usage pattern emerges (students checking queue daily). Follow-up response rates measurable. Users describe Offerloop as "my outreach system" not "that email finder."
- **Overall:** User retention improves. NPS improves. Word-of-mouth referrals increase. Students tell other students "you need this."

## Dependencies

- Phase 1 (email quality) must be validated before Phase 2 begins. The edit rate metric is the gate.
- Gmail integration must be solid for Phase 2 (send flow) and airtight for Phase 3 (automated follow-ups)
- Job data quality needs improvement for the recommended jobs feed in Phase 2
- Onboarding may need a redesign to capture richer student profile data

## The Assignment

Before you build anything in Phase 2, do this: pick 10 of your current paying users. Send them 3-5 emails generated by the new system (after your co-founder's improvements). Ask one question: "Would you send this as-is, or would you change it?" Track exactly what they change and why. That's your edit rate baseline. If it's above 40% send-as-is, start building the pipeline. If it's below, keep iterating on email quality. The emails are the foundation. Don't build the house until the foundation is solid.
